CREMA / GHANA / PUBLICATION DESIGN & DOCUMENTARY WORK

From the printed page
to the community.

I designed constitutions for Community Resource Management Areas in Ghana and worked as the project’s photographer, graphic designer and videographer. This is the story of the publications and the community engagement I helped document.

My contribution
Constitution design, graphic design, photography and videography
Organisation
Forestry Commission of Ghana
Wildlife Division
Project timeline
Constitutions: October 2018
Dissemination: 29 May to 11 June 2019

TOOLS I USED

Adobe InDesignAdobe PhotoshopAdobe LightroomAdobe Premiere Pro

WHERE THE WORK STARTED

A constitution with a place in everyday life.

These publications were part of a wider effort to support communities in managing their natural resources. The constitutions set out how each CREMA would operate, from its purpose and membership to the rules around resource use.

I designed the covers and page layouts, bringing the constitution content into a consistent visual format across the collection.

The five documents in this collection are for Akontombraman, Aowin, Asuo Bia Nkyirima, Boin and Juaboso District. Their covers share a green and white palette while retaining each CREMA’s name, emblem and motto where included.

THE CONSTITUTION COLLECTION

A family of designs, each with its own identity.

I designed each constitution around its CREMA’s name, emblem and shared conservation purpose.

BEYOND THE COVER

The structure inside.

The design continues through the interior: numbered sections, bold headings, a running header and the CREMA emblem. This page shows how the name, aim, motto and purpose sit within the document.

The layout carries the visual identity from the cover into the document.

TAKING THE WORK INTO THE FIELD

The pages were part of a bigger conversation.

In May and June 2019, I joined the Wildlife Division team as we took the constitution and bye-law programme into the communities. My work moved from designing the publications to documenting the people and conversations around them.

Together, we visited all 21 Community Resource Management Committees across Asuo Bia Nkyirima, Akontombraman and Juaboso District.

We met chiefs, elders, queen mothers, committee members and residents. Because many participants were farmers, the sessions were arranged around times that suited their working day.

MY CAMERA, THE TEAM’S WORK

Recording the people behind the programme.

Alongside my design work, I photographed the project and worked as its videographer. Through my camera, I captured the gatherings, presentations and smaller conversations taking place around the programme.

The discussions brought practical questions into the room: protecting farms from wildlife, understanding permits and knowing the responsibilities of committee members. Colleagues led the presentations and answered those questions. I documented those exchanges through my photography and video work.

The programme also included separate discussions about additional or alternative livelihoods, led by a colleague. My photograph below shows one of those conversations at Abronehia.

LOOKING BACK

The connection that stays with me.

Looking back at the publications and photographs together, what stands out is the connection between a designed document and the people it concerns. This project brought my publication design, photography and video work into the same community setting.

Despite difficult roads to some communities, we completed the planned committee visits. My photographs bring that journey into this portfolio, connecting the constitution designs with the people and places behind them.
